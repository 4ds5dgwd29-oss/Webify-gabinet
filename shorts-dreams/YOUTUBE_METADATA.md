## Part 2 — `dreams_part2_why_your_brain_believes_dreams.mp4` (41.8 s)
**Title:** Why Your Brain Believes Even the Weirdest Dreams 🧠 #shorts

**Description:**
Your childhood home in your school? A friend turning into someone else? And somehow, you accept it.
During REM sleep the prefrontal cortex (logic, planning, self-doubt) gets quieter, while the amygdala (emotion) becomes highly active. The critic goes silent and emotions take over.

## Part 3 — `dreams_part3_why_dreams_feel_so_real.mp4` (36.3 s)
**Title:** Why Dreams Feel SO Real 🌙 #shorts

**Description:**
Your brain builds a dream with the same tools it uses when you are awake. Visual areas recognize shapes, faces and places with your eyes closed, and movement areas make running, falling and flying feel physical.
Some scientists think dreams help us process emotions and rehearse challenges in a place where nothing can hurt us.
Part 3 of "How Dreams Work".

## Part 4 — `dreams_part4_why_you_forget_your_dreams.mp4` (38.4 s)
**Title:** Why You Forget Your Dreams (and how to remember them) ⏰ #shorts

**Description:**
Within minutes of waking, most of a dream is gone. During REM sleep, levels of noradrenaline (a chemical that helps store new memories) drop very low, so the dream is experienced but often never saved.
Tip: when you wake up, stay still, keep your eyes closed, replay the dream, then write it down.
Part 4 of "How Dreams Work".
